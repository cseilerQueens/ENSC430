ENSC 430A/B

Honours Project in Environmental Sustainability (2026/27)

Course Description

Climate change is one of the defining challenges of the 21st century, influencing ecosystems, biodiversity, food production, water resources, human health, and many other aspects of the environment. This two-term capstone course provides ENVS and ENSC majors with the opportunity to synthesize knowledge gained throughout their undergraduate studies and apply it to real-world climate change questions. Rather than introducing climate change as a stand-alone topic, the course examines environmental processes through the lens of climate change and equips students with the quantitative tools needed to investigate them. During the fall term, students develop a toolkit for evidence-based climate assessment. In the winter term, students work in small groups on a climate-related research project of their choice. They combine the concepts and analytical approaches introduced during the fall term to investigate a real-world problem, such as the impacts of climate change on biodiversity, wildfires, drought, food systems, or climate adaptation and mitigation strategies. The course concludes with a public Project Symposium, where students present their findings to peers and faculty.

ENSC 430A Fall Term

Week Module Topic
1 Climate Change A 200-year journey from science to policy
2 Understanding Environmental Systems (I) Dynamical systems, feedbacks, and resilience
3 Understanding Environmental Systems (II) Causal inference and identifying drivers of change
4 Climate Projections and Impacts (I) Climate extremes and analysis of future climate risks
5 Climate Projections and Impacts (II) Climate change impacts on biodiversity
6 Climate Projections and Impacts (III) Attributing environmental changes using climate data and causal methods
7 Observing Environmental Change (I) Satellite observations for ecosystem monitoring
8 Observing Environmental Change (II) Reanalysis data and historical climate assessment
9 Observing Environmental Change (III) Ecosystem responses from in situ observations and flux measurements
10 Climate Futures and Solutions (I) Integrated assessment models and pathways for mitigation
11 Climate Futures and Solutions (II) Earth system models and long-term climate trajectories
12 Capstone Project Developing Research Questions and Forming Project Teams

ENSC 430B Winter Term

Week Module Topic
1 Project Definition Define research question and project objectives
2 Project Design Select datasets and analytical methods
3 Background Research Review the scientific literature and refine the research question
4 Research Planning Develop and refine the project plan
5 Project Proposal Present research plan (10 minutes per group)
6 Data Analysis Begin data acquisition and analysis
7 Data Analysis Continue analysis and refine methodology
8 Interpretation Interpret results and assess uncertainties
9 Scientific Communication Draft project report
10 Scientific Communication Prepare symposium presentation
11 Knowledge Dissemination Present findings at the Project Symposium
12 Project Completion Revise and finalize the project report

Tutorials

During Weeks 1-11, each student will complete a tutorial report and answer a set of questions. Both components are required to earn the associated points. The tutorial report consists of a PDF file submitted through onQ. The questions must be answered directly in onQ and should not be included in the tutorial report.
